Stargazing Basics

How to Prepare

  • Check moon phase — new moon is best for deep-sky
  • Use a sky app: Stellarium, SkySafari, Star Walk
  • Let your eyes adapt for 20–30 minutes

Understanding the Sky

  • Winter: Orion, Taurus, Gemini
  • Spring: Leo, Virgo
  • Summer: Cygnus, Lyra, Scorpius
  • Autumn: Pegasus, Andromeda

Star-hopping: Start at Alnitak → Flame Nebula → Horsehead Nebula.

What to Observe

  • Planets: Jupiter, Saturn, Mars
  • Nebulae: Orion, Lagoon
  • Star clusters: Pleiades, Beehive
  • Galaxies: Andromeda

Choosing a Telescope

What to Consider

  • Aperture is king — more light = better views
  • Planets: 70–90 mm
  • Galaxies/nebulae: 130–150 mm
  • Deep-sky: 200 mm+

Mount Types

  • Alt‑az: simple
  • Equatorial: tracks sky
  • Dobsonian: best value

Telescope Types

  • Refractors: sharp, low-maintenance
  • Reflectors: best value
  • Maksutov: compact, great for planets

Astrophotography Tips

Essential Techniques

Use the 300 rule: exposure ≈ 300 / focal length.

  • ISO 800–1600 is ideal
  • Shoot in RAW
  • Stack images for best results

Deep-Sky Imaging

  • Use darks, flats, bias frames
  • Guiding allows 2–5 minute exposures

Planetary Imaging

  • Record video at 100–200 fps
  • Stack sharp frames
  • Sharpen with wavelets
